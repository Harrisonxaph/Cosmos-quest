# Roadmap

1. **Foundation (done):** schema-validated facts, citations, licence inventory, mirrored tests, CI and Pages workflow.
2. **Planet exploration and learning (done):** a Three.js 3D explorer with the Sun and the eight planets, smooth camera flights, rotate and zoom, pause, keyboard and touch controls, reduced-motion behaviour and a clearly labelled illustrative scale. Information panels show NASA measurements, dated moon counts, composition and facts, with simple and detailed modes and comparisons with Earth. A 2D fallback keeps everything usable without 3D. Covered by unit tests and browser tests. At the owner's request this stage also delivered the fact panels first planned for stage 3.
3. **Planet Quiz (done):** a short, encouraging quiz. Each round has five different multiple-choice questions with three answers, drawn from 27 questions that only use facts the app already cites. Each answer gets an explanation and a NASA source link, the child can explore that world and come back without losing their place, and the round ends with results, "Play again" and "Back to exploring". No timers, penalties, accounts or tracking. Works with keyboard, touch, screen readers and without 3D. Covered by unit and browser tests.
4. **Guided Discovery Missions (done):** three missions (*Meet our rocky neighbours*, *Giants without ground*, *Understanding space*) with an introduction, 3–5 numbered steps, deliberate “I’ve found it” or answer actions (opening a world never counts by itself), sourced explanations, encouraging feedback and a recap with replay or explore freely. Leave any time and resume for this visit; mission and quiz progress are independent. No accounts, tracking, timers, rewards or audio. Works with keyboard, touch, screen readers and without 3D. Covered by unit and browser tests.
5. **More activities (not started, not agreed):** experiments or further missions, each using only cited content and with feature tests.
5. **Release review:** check facts against primary sources again, inspect every asset licence, and test on real phones and tablets, with a keyboard and with a screen reader.

Ideas for later, not yet agreed: real NASA imagery as planet textures (each with a licence record), planet positions for today's date, major moons in 3D, and offline (PWA) support.
